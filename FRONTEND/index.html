      03 · Motion & physics

      Speed, acceleration, jerk — and energy.

      Hand trajectories carry information that a still image cannot. The curves in the first two panels are illustrative: a smooth idealised movement, not measured data. Real measurements appear in Live motion analytics once the backend sends landmark data.

      Theoretical minimum-jerk movement over 1 s. Axes are fixed so you can see curves grow as speed increases.

      Kinetic energy

      Theoretical
      KE = ½ · m · v²

      Energy grows with the square of speed: doubling speed quadruples energy. Fast and slow versions of the same shape can therefore look very different.

      04 · Our approach

      Shape alone is not enough.

      Many signs share a hand shape and differ only in how they move. Sankeet Shayak combines both. This is a conceptual comparison — not a measured result.

      05 · About the project

      Built to make signing understood.

      Problem

      Sign languages depend on both hand shape and movement, yet many recognition systems look at shape alone and behave like opaque black boxes. Communication between signers and non-signers remains difficult.

      Objectives

      • Track hands from ordinary video using landmarks
      • Derive geometry, joint angles and kinematics
      • Estimate physics-inspired dynamic features
      • Classify signs with an interpretable pipeline

      Architecture & tech

      Video inputMediaPipe HandsJoint anglesKinematicsDynamic featuresML classifier

      Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript. Recognition backend: to be integrated.

      Advantages

      • Uses motion as well as shape
      • Features can be explained and inspected
      • Works from a standard camera

      Limitations

      • Classifier results are pending experimental validation
      • Depends on lighting and hand visibility
      • Physics features are estimated from 2D video

      Applications & future scope

      Proposed, not yet demonstrated

      • Assistive communication tools
      • Sign-language learning aids
      • Larger vocabularies and continuous signing
